WHAT THIS IS
------------
A relational database of every ruler of China from Qin Shi Huang (221 BCE)
to the abdication of Puyi (12 February 1912 CE), built for the Week 4
assignment: "a real database, and a progressive skill". Every row carries a
source (document + juan) and a note for anything uncertain. Built entirely
through opencode, which explained each step as it went.

WHAT WAS BUILT
--------------
  3 tables, 3 foreign keys, 359 rows in total:

    dynasties   44 rows   states from Qin 秦 to Qing 清, with years, capitals
    rulers     280 rows   every sovereign, Chinese original beside the
                          romanised name, temple/posthumous names, reign
                          years, predecessor link
    era_names   35 rows   年号, keyed to rulers (full for Ming/Qing,
                          selected icons for Han/Tang/Song)

  Foreign keys:
    rulers.dynasty_id      -> dynasties.id
    rulers.predecessor_id  -> rulers.id        (self-reference)
    era_names.ruler_id     -> rulers.id

  Rebuildable: python code/build_db.py  (foreign keys ON, seeds in order,
  never edit old seed files — add a new one and rerun).

SCOPE AND COUNTING RULES (full version in research/design/design_doc.md)
------------------------------------------------------------------------
  - Time range: 221 BCE to 1912 CE. Shang and Zhou kings are out of scope
    (they were kings 王, not emperors 皇帝 — the title begins with
    Qin Shi Huang in 221 BCE).
  - Included: rival states in divided periods, deposed and short-reign
    rulers, child emperors, Wu Zetian (her own state Wu Zhou 690-705).
  - Excluded: regents who never took the title (Empress Lü, Dorgon, Cixi).
  - Date convention: integers, negative = BCE, no year 0.
  - The result: 280 rulers across 44 states — the answer to "how many
    emperors" depends entirely on the counting rules, which is the point.

FINDINGS WORTH SHOWING IN THE DEMO
----------------------------------
  1. Longest reigns: Kangxi 玄烨 61 years; Qianlong 弘历 61 (abdicated 1796,
     kept power to 1799); Han Wudi 刘彻 54.
  2. Shortest: Wanyan Chenglin 完颜承麟 — made emperor in the morning the
     Jurchen Jin fell, dead by the afternoon (1234).
  3. Youngest: Liu Long 刘隆 enthroned ~100 days old (106 CE); Puyi at 2
     (1908).
  4. Succession crosses dynasties: Cao Pi's predecessor is Han Xiandi, Li
     Yuan's is Yang Tong, Zhu Wen's is the last Tang boy — the "abdication
     coup" is a repeatable pattern the predecessor_id chain makes visible.
     23 cross-dynasty links, by design.
  5. Name traps (critical for the quiz questions): three emperors romanised
     Liu Yu (刘裕 / 刘彧 / 刘昱), two Sima Yan (司马炎 / 司马衍), two Zhao Xu
     (赵顼 / 赵煦), two Li Heng (李亨 / 李恒), two Xuanzong (玄宗 / 宣宗),
     two Huizong (宋徽宗 / 元惠宗).
  6. Kings vs emperors: most Ten Kingdoms rulers were kings 王; Ziying,
     Ruzi Ying and Tianshundi are flagged rows that a naive count would miss
     or miscount.

ERRORS FOUND AND FIXED ALONG THE WAY (candidate improvement-log entries)
------------------------------------------------------------------------
  1. UNIQUE constraint failed on dynasties.name — two different states both
     named "Wu" (Three Kingdoms 吳 and Ten Kingdoms 楊吳). Fixed by renaming
     the latter Yang Wu.
  2. UTF-8 mojibake: editing seed files through a PowerShell
     Get-Content/Set-Content round-trip read the files in the ANSI codepage
     and corrupted every Chinese character in two seed files
     (赵匡胤 -> èµµåŒ¡èƒ¤). Caught by the verify script's Chinese queries
     returning nothing. Fixed by rewriting both files in the editor; added
     code/check_encoding.py which now scans every text column (0 bad
     cells). Rule: never round-trip UTF-8 files through shell string ops.
  3. Zhu Wen's predecessor link was NULL; set to the last Tang emperor
     (id 139) so "who followed Li Zhu" now correctly returns 朱温.

  Console note: print Chinese with PYTHONIOENCODING=utf-8 on this machine
  (the default cp1252 console crashes on 汉字).
